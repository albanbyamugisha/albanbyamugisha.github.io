Last week I watched AI generate a "working" function in seconds. Clean syntax. No errors. Passed the first test.

It also missed the actual problem.

- It didn't ask why the data was structured that way.
- It didn't flag that the "fix" would break three other modules down the line.
- It didn't push back and say, "are you sure this is the right approach?"

**That's the part nobody talks about enough.**

> AI is brilliant at producing code. It has no idea what the code is for.

It doesn't understand your users. It doesn't know the trade-off between shipping fast and shipping right. It can't sit in a meeting and sense that the requirements everyone agreed on don't actually solve the real problem.

**That's still on us.**

This changes how I think about what I'm actually learning. The syntax was never the hard part. The hard part is judgement: knowing what to build, what to question, and what to leave alone.

AI is going to keep getting better at writing code. Fine. Let it.

The engineers who'll matter most won't be the ones who can out-code AI. They'll be the ones who can out-think it. The ones asking the questions AI doesn't even know to ask.

Learn the tools. Use them well. But don't outsource the thinking.

What's one thing about engineering AI still can't replace for you?
